Academic Capstone Research Draft — October 2026 
Abstract— Machine learning models in financial forecasting frequently fail to preserve portfolio capital because 
directional predictions are disconnected from dynamic risk management. This study conducts a rigorous retrospective 
walk-forward evaluation of a Two-Tier Computational Intelligence (CI) architecture across three large-cap Indian 
equities (RELIANCE.NS, TCS.NS, INFY.NS) over a 6-year period (2020–2025, 1,487 trading sessions per asset). Tier 1 
employs a LightGBM gradient-boosted decision tree to estimate forward 3-day directional probabilities from 20 
technical indicators. Tier 2 leverages the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) to evolve real-valued 
trading rule chromosomes (probability hurdles, dynamic ATR stop-loss and take-profit multipliers, trailing stops, and 
regime-conditional capital allocation) simultaneously optimizing Annualized Sharpe Ratio and Maximum Drawdown. All 
backtests enforce strict next-bar Open execution, slippage, transaction costs (10 bps round-trip standard; 40 bps 
sensitivity), conservative intrabar bracket conflict ordering, horizon purging, and scheduled fold-end liquidation 
prohibiting final-day entries. Across 18 annual test folds and three GA seeds, LightGBM achieves a modest out-of-sample 
directional AUC of 0.5324 and accuracy of 52.68% (against the training-prior baseline of 52.66%). The Full Hybrid 
strategy dampens annual maximum drawdown relative to Buy & Hold in 17 out of 18 folds (94.4%). However, by 
remaining in cash during secular market expansions, the Hybrid underperforms Buy & Hold total return in 13 out of 18 
folds (72.2%), delivering multi-year stitched seed-median returns of -0.35% for RELIANCE.NS, +25.60% for TCS.NS, and 
+9.10% for INFY.NS, compared to Buy & Hold compound growth of +128.74%, +65.13%, and +152.78%, respectively. 
An evolutionary ablation without machine learning signals yields comparable or superior multi-year returns. These 
results demonstrate that defensive metaheuristic rules provide robust tail-risk dampening but impose substantial 
opportunity costs in trending equity markets. 
Keywords— Computational Intelligence, Algorithmic Trading, LightGBM, NSGA-II, Multi-Objective Optimization, Walk-
Forward Validation, National Stock Exchange of India (NSE), Backtest Overfitting. 
1. Introduction & Research Question 
The application of machine learning (ML) to financial market forecasting has expanded rapidly, yet practical deployment 
consistently confronts the realities of low signal-to-noise ratios, non-stationarity, and market efficiency [1], [5]. A central 
failure mode in computational finance literature is the conceptual gap between directional statistical prediction and 
dynamic capital preservation. A predictive classifier achieving 55% directional accuracy will rapidly deplete portfolio 
equity if trade exits, transaction friction, and volatility-induced position sizing are left unmanaged [3]. Conversely, 
traditional rule-based algorithmic strategies rely on static technical indicators that fail to adapt across changing volatility 
regimes. 
To bridge this gap, this study investigates a Two-Tier Computational Intelligence (CI) architecture that decouples 
directional statistical inference from multi-objective policy search. Tier 1 trains a gradient-boosted decision tree 
(LightGBM) to estimate forward 3-day directional return probabilities from engineered technical and volatility features. 
Tier 2 applies the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) [1] to evolve continuous trading rule 
parameters—including entry/exit probability thresholds, Average True Range (ATR) stop-loss and take-profit multipliers, 
trailing stop percentages, and regime-conditioned capital allocations. By framing strategy selection as a bi-objective 
optimization between risk-adjusted return (Sharpe Ratio) and capital risk (Maximum Drawdown), the evolutionary 
engine constructs a Pareto frontier of candidate trading policies.

2. Related Work & Theoretical Foundations 
The theoretical foundation of empirical market forecasting rests upon the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) 
formulated by Fama [6], which posits that historical prices rapidly reflect available information, leaving technical trading 
rules with negligible risk-adjusted excess returns net of transaction costs. In modern econometric literature, White [5] 
established that empirical outperformance in trading rule evaluations is frequently an artifact of data snooping bias: 
searching across thousands of parameter permutations guarantees finding strategies that appear profitable purely due 
to random noise. Bailey and López de Prado [4] formalized this hazard via the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR), 
demonstrating that the expected maximum Sharpe ratio increases as a function of the number of evaluated trials and 
non-normal return distributions. 
To mitigate backtest overfitting, López de Prado [3] proposed rigorous protocols for financial machine learning, notably 
purged and embargoed combinatorial cross-validation. When overlapping forward labels are utilized (e.g., multi-bar 
holding returns), standard random cross-validation leaks information across adjacent folds. Purging trailing samples 
equal to the prediction horizon prevents this forward-looking contamination. 
In the domain of multi-objective metaheuristics, Deb et al. [1] introduced NSGA-II, which eliminated the $O(M N^3)$ 
computational bottlenecks of early genetic algorithms by introducing fast non-dominated sorting ($O(M N^2)$), elitist 
selection preserving non-dominated individuals across generations, and parameter-less crowding distance operators. In 
tree-based machine learning, Ke et al. [2] developed LightGBM, combining Gradient-based One-Side Sampling (GOSS) 
and Exclusive Feature Bundling (EFB) to provide high-speed, scalable gradient boosting on tabular data. Our work 
integrates these two established paradigms into an auditable, causal walk-forward backtesting framework. 
3. Methodology & System Architecture 
The proposed CI system operates across two distinct tiers coupled to a vectorized causal execution simulator. Figure 1 
illustrates the end-to-end data processing, feature engineering, model training, evolutionary search, and test evaluation 
pipeline. 
3.1 Tier 1: Feature Engineering & Predictive Classifier 
From historical daily OHLCV series, the system extracts 20 stationary technical and volatility features spanning four core 
categories: (1) Momentum: Relative Strength Index (RSI-14), Stochastic %K and %D, Rate of Change (ROC-5, ROC-20), 
and Williams %R; (2) Trend: MACD line, MACD signal, MACD histogram, and price ratios to 20-day, 50-day, and 200-day 
Simple Moving Averages; (3) Volatility: Average True Range percentage (ATR-14 / Close), Bollinger Bands %B and 
Bandwidth, and Historical Volatility (20-day return standard deviation); (4) Volume: On-Balance Volume (OBV) 20-day

slope, Chaikin Money Flow (CMF-20), and Volume SMA ratios. Additionally, a market volatility regime filter classifies 
sessions where 20-day annualized historical volatility exceeds its expanding 60-day median. 
The prediction target is formulated as a binary directional indicator over a 3-day holding horizon: $y_t = 1$ if $(C_{t+3} - 
C_t)/C_t > 0$, and $y_t = 0$ otherwise. A LightGBM classifier is trained on historical data with hyperparameters: 
max_depth=5, num_leaves=24, learning_rate=0.03, n_estimators=300, and early stopping on a separate validation 
partition. LightGBM outputs calibrated conditional probabilities $hat{p}_t = hat{P}(y_t = 1 | {x}_t)$. 
3.2 Tier 2: NSGA-II Multi-Objective Evolutionary Engine 
The evolutionary decision layer optimizes a 7-dimensional real-valued chromosome ${g} = [p_{{entry}}, p_{{exit}}, {SL}, 
{TP}, {Trail}, {VolAlloc}, {MaxHold}]$ bounded by domain constraints: $p_{{entry}} in [0.52, 0.75]$, $p_{{exit}} in [0.35, 
0.50]$, Stop-Loss multiplier ${SL} in [1.0, 5.0] x {ATR}$, Take-Profit multiplier ${TP} in [1.5, 10.0] x {ATR}$, Trailing Stop 
percentage ${Trail} in [0.01, 0.10]$, Volatility discount allocation fraction ${VolAlloc} in [0.20, 1.00]$, and Maximum 
holding duration ${MaxHold} in [3, 45]$ bars. 
NSGA-II evaluates each candidate individual across the 2-year Development partition using two competing objective 
functions: (1) Maximize Annualized Sharpe Ratio f_1 = (mu_r / sigma_r) * sqrt(252) (assuming a 0.0% risk-free rate); (2) 
Minimize Maximum Drawdown f_2 = max_t ((peak_t - E_t) / peak_t). To enforce behavioral validity, a feasibility 
constraint requires candidate rules to generate at least 5 completed trades during development. Individuals with $<5$ 
trades are penalized with $f_1 = -10.0$ and $f_2 = 1.0$. The population of size $N=30$ evolves across $G=10$ 
generations using Simulated Binary Crossover (SBX, $eta_c = 15$, $p_c = 0.90$) and Polynomial Mutation ($eta_m = 
20$, $p_m = 1/7$). 
3.3 Causal Simulator & Execution Mechanics 
The backtest engine enforces realistic execution timing and friction: (1) Next-Bar Execution: Signals evaluated at bar $t$ 
Close execute strictly at bar $t+1$ Open at price $P_{{entry}} = O_{t+1}(1 + {slip})$; (2) Transaction Friction: Standard 
costs are fixed at 0.05% commission and 0.05% slippage per side (10 bps round-trip); sensitivity tests double friction to 
20 bps per side (40 bps round-trip); (3) Conservative Intrabar Conflict: If both stop-loss and take-profit thresholds are 
touched during the same session's high-low range, the simulator prioritizes the stop-loss; (4) Opening Gaps: Gaps below 
stop-loss or above take-profit execute at the opening price with slippage penalty; (5) Zero-Volume Prohibition: No trade 
fills occur on zero-volume sessions; (6) Scheduled Fold-End Policy: New entries are prohibited on the scheduled last 
tradable day ($i < {last_tradable_idx}$); open positions liquidate at final session Close.

5.3 Annual Paired Strategy Differences & Scorecard 
Evaluating the 18 annual folds individually reveals the structural asymmetry between annual drawdown dampening and 
secular compounding: (1) Positive Hybrid Return: Achieved in 10 out of 18 folds (55.6%); (2) Return Outperformance vs 
Buy & Hold: Hybrid exceeded Buy & Hold in 5 out of 18 folds (27.8%)—specifically during market stress in 2022 (TCS 
+16.55% diff, INFY +11.44% diff), 2023 (RELIANCE +2.00% diff, TCS +3.92% diff), and 2024 (RELIANCE +1.97% diff); (3) 
Buy & Hold Outperformance: Buy & Hold exceeded Hybrid in 13 out of 18 folds (72.2%), particularly during strong bull 
markets such as 2020 (INFY B&H +74.69% vs Hyb +27.83%) and 2021 (INFY B&H +52.73% vs Hyb +17.22%); (4) Maximum 
Drawdown Reduction: Hybrid exhibited lower annual maximum drawdown in 17 out of 18 folds (94.4%). 
5.4 Ablation and Exposure-Matched Diagnostic 
To isolate the contribution of the ML classifier, a Constant Probability Ablation ($p=0.60$) evolved rules omitting 
LightGBM features. The Ablation achieved 6-year stitched returns of +42.42% for RELIANCE.NS (vs Hyb -0.35%), +47.37% 
for TCS.NS (vs Hyb +25.60%), and +18.69% for INFY.NS (vs Hyb +9.10%). Furthermore, an Exposure -Matched Buy & Hold 
diagnostic blending passive holding with cash based on the Hybrid's development exposure fraction ($w_{{dev}}$)

5.5 Transaction Cost Sensitivity & Slippage Dynamics 
Evaluating frozen Development chromosomes under doubled transaction friction (20 bps per side, 40 bps round -trip) 
caused substantial performance drops: RELIANCE.NS Seed 42 total return declined from -6.58% to -25.41% (-18.83 pp); 
TCS.NS Seed 43 declined from +25.60% to +19.61% (-5.99 pp); and INFY.NS Seed 44 dropped from +27.88% to -7.97% (-
35.85 pp). Mechanistically, slippage does not act as a linear cost subtraction. Because execution price shifts upward 
($P_{{entry}} = O(1+{slip})$), the dynamic ATR stop-loss threshold ($P_{{stop}} = P_{{entry}} - {SL} x {ATR}$) is 
mechanically elevated. On sessions where prices touch the stop band, trades exit prematurely, altering subsequent cash 
levels and eliminating future trade signals.

6. Discussion, Risk Attribution & Methodological Limitations 
The empirical findings highlight several critical trade-offs in quantitative portfolio design: (1) Asymmetric Risk-Return 
Profile: NSGA-II successfully discovers defensive chromosome parameters that truncate downside drawdowns during 
sharp corrections. However, because the classifier's directional probabilities hover narrowly around 0.50, high entry 
thresholds ($p_{{entry}} >= 0.55$) cause the system to remain in cash during multi-month bull market regimes, resulting 
in heavy compounding penalties relative to passive index compounding. (2) Capital Exposure as an Overnight Proxy: In 
our evaluation, capital exposure is defined as session-close invested value divided by equity (${Shares}_t x C_t / E_t$). 
This proxy reflects overnight capital commitment and omits intraday capital utilization for same-day round trips. (3) 
Survivorship and Single-Asset Constraints: The study evaluates three surviving large-cap equities individually. Results 
cannot be generalized to broad cross-sectional universes where portfolio diversification, cross-asset rebalancing, and 
delisting survivorship bias operate. (4) Retrospective Protocol Adjustments: While data snapshots and random seeds 
remained strictly frozen, the final experimental protocol incorporated retrospective corrections (versioned in 
protocol_correction.json v1.1.0) following initial simulator inspection. True out-of-sample validity requires prospective 
forward execution. 
7. Conclusion 
This research evaluated the integration of gradient tree boosting (LightGBM) with multi-objective evolutionary 
optimization (NSGA-II) for algorithmic trading across three major Indian equities over 2020–2025. Across 18 annual 
walk-forward folds, the LightGBM classifier demonstrated modest predictive capability (AUC 0.5324), tracking majority-

superior capital growth. These results provide an honest empirical caution against assuming that machine learning 
directional predictors consistently improve quantitative trading strategies, highlighting the paramount role of 
opportunity cost in risk-constrained portfolio systems.
