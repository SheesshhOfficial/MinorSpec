Risk-Return Trade-offs of LightGBM and NSGA-II Trading Rules: 
A Retrospective Walk-Forward Study of Three NSE Equities 
Ashish Ranjan,  Khuushi Maheshwari,  Satyanarayan H. 
Department of Computer Science, Manipal Institute of Technology 
B.Tech in Computer Science 
Draft Academic Manuscript — October 2026 
Abstract— Machine learning models in financial forecasting frequently fail to preserve portfolio capital because directional 
predictions are disconnected from dynamic risk management. This study conducts a retrospective walk-forward evaluation of 
a Two-Tier Computational Intelligence (CI) architecture across three large-cap Indian equities (RELIANCE.NS, TCS.NS, INFY.NS) 
over a 6-year period (2020–2025, 1,487 trading sessions per asset). Tier 1 employs a LightGBM gradient-boosted decision tree 
to estimate forward 3-day directional return probabilities from 20 technical indicators. Tier 2 leverages the Non-dominated 
Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) to evolve real-valued trading rule chromosomes (probability hurdles, dynamic ATR 
stop-loss and take-profit multipliers, trailing stops, and volatility-conditioned position sizing) simultaneously optimizing 
Annualized Sharpe Ratio and Maximum Drawdown. All backtests enforce strict next-bar Open execution, transaction costs 
(0.05% commission + 0.05% slippage per side, approximately 10 bps per side and 20 bps round-trip; 40 bps sensitivity), 
conservative intrabar bracket conflict ordering, horizon purging, and scheduled fold-end liquidation barring final-day entries. 
Across 18 annual test folds and three GA seeds, LightGBM achieves a modest out-of-sample directional AUC of 0.5324 and 
accuracy of 52.68% (against the training-prior baseline of 52.66%). The Full Hybrid strategy dampens annual maximum 
drawdown relative to Buy & Hold in 17 out of 18 folds (94.4%). However, by remaining in cash during secular market 
expansions, the Hybrid underperforms Buy & Hold total return in 13 out of 18 folds (72.2%), delivering multi-year stitched 
seed-median returns of -0.35% for RELIANCE.NS, +25.60% for TCS.NS, and +9.10% for INFY.NS, compared to Buy & Hold 
compound growth of +128.74%, +65.13%, and +152.78%, respectively. An evolutionary ablation without machine learning 
signals yields comparable or higher multi-year returns. These results demonstrate that defensive metaheuristic rules provide 
robust tail-risk dampening in corrective periods but impose substantial opportunity costs in trending equity markets. 
Keywords— Computational Intelligence, Algorithmic Trading, LightGBM, NSGA-II, Multi-Objective Optimization, Walk-
Forward Validation, National Stock Exchange of India (NSE), Backtest Overfitting. 
1. Introduction & Research Question 
The application of machine learning (ML) to financial market forecasting has expanded rapidly, yet practical deployment 
consistently confronts the realities of low signal-to-noise ratios, non-stationarity, and market efficiency [3], [5], [6]. A central 
failure mode in computational finance literature is the conceptual gap between directional statistical prediction and dynamic 
capital preservation. A predictive classifier achieving 55% directional accuracy will rapidly deplete portfolio equity if trade 
exits, transaction friction, and volatility-induced position sizing are unmanaged [3]. Conversely, traditional rule-based 
algorithmic strategies rely on static technical indicators that fail to adapt across changing volatility regimes. 
To investigate this trade-off, this study examines a Two-Tier Computational Intelligence (CI) architecture that decouples 
directional statistical inference from multi-objective policy search. Tier 1 trains a gradient-boosted decision tree (LightGBM) to 
estimate forward 3-day directional return probabilities from engineered technical indicators. Tier 2 applies the Non-dominated 
Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) [1] to evolve continuous trading rule parameters—including entry/exit probability 
thresholds, Average True Range (ATR) stop-loss and take-profit multipliers, trailing stop percentages, and regime-conditioned 
capital allocations. By framing strategy selection as a bi-objective optimization between risk-adjusted return (Sharpe Ratio) 
and capital risk (Maximum Drawdown), the evolutionary engine constructs a Pareto frontier of candidate trading policies. 
We ground our investigation in three specific research questions: (1) Does gradient tree boosting provide measurable 
directional predictive edge on large-cap National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) equities over simple training priors? (2) Does 
evolving multi-objective risk parameters dampen out-of-sample portfolio drawdowns during historical market downturns? (3) 
How does cash preservation under defensive probability hurdles affect multi-year compounded capital accumulation relative 
to passive benchmark holding?

2. Related Work & Theoretical Foundations 
The theoretical foundation of empirical market predictability is rooted in the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) formulated by 
Fama [6], which posits that historical prices reflect available trading information, providing the benchmark against which 
technical trading strategies must be judged. In modern econometrics, Halbert White [5] established that empirical 
outperformance in trading rule evaluations is frequently an artifact of data snooping bias: searching across numerous 
parameter permutations risks selecting rules that appear profitable purely due to random chance. Bailey and López de Prado 
[4] formalized this hazard via the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR), demonstrating that observed Sharpe ratios must be evaluated 
in light of sample size, return non-normality, and the effective number of strategy trials. 
To mitigate backtest overfitting, López de Prado [3] proposed rigorous protocols for financial machine learning, notably purged 
and embargoed cross-validation. When overlapping forward labels are utilized (e.g., multi-bar holding returns), standard 
random cross-validation leaks information across adjacent folds. Purging trailing samples equal to the prediction horizon 
prevents this forward-looking contamination. 
In the domain of multi-objective metaheuristics, Deb et al. [1] introduced NSGA-II, which eliminated the high computational 
complexity of early genetic algorithms through fast non-dominated sorting, elitist parent-offspring combining, and a 
parameter-less crowding distance operator that preserves population diversity. In tabular machine learning, Ke et al. [2] 
developed LightGBM, combining Gradient-based One-Side Sampling (GOSS) and Exclusive Feature Bundling (EFB) to provide 
high-speed, scalable gradient boosting. Our work integrates these established paradigms into an auditable, causal walk-
forward backtesting framework.

3.1 Tier 1: Feature Engineering & Predictive Classifier 
From historical daily OHLCV series, the feature pipeline computes 20 technical and regime indicators: (1) Trend Distances: 
percentage distances to 10-day, 50-day, and 200-day Simple Moving Averages (dist_sma_10, dist_sma_50, dist_sma_200); (2) 
Momentum Indicators: MACD ratio (macd / close), MACD histogram, 14-day RSI (rsi_14), Stochastic %K and %D (stoch_k, 
stoch_d), and Rate of Change over 5, 10, and 20 days (roc_5, roc_10, roc_20); (3) Volatility Indicators: ATR percentage (atr_ 14 
/ close), 20-day and 60-day annualized realized return volatility (volatility_20, volatility_60), Bollinger Bands %B (bb_pct_b), 
and Bandwidth (bb_width); (4) Volume Indicators: 20-day volume z-score (volume_zscore) and 5-day On-Balance Volume Rate 
of Change (obv_roc_5); (5) Market Regimes: Volatility Regime flag (regime_high_vol = 1 if volatility_20 exceeds its rolling 100-
day median, else 0) and Trend Regime flag (regime_trend = 1 if Close > SMA50 > SMA200; -1 if Close < SMA50 < SMA200; 0 
otherwise). No stationarity transformations or formal unit root tests are claimed; features are evaluated directly as continuous 
inputs. 
The supervised prediction target is a binary directional indicator over a 3-day holding horizon: y_t = 1 if (Close_{t+3} - Close_t) 
/ Close_t > 0, and y_t = 0 otherwise. A LightGBM classifier is trained on historical data with parameters: 
num_boost_round=250, learning_rate=0.03, max_depth=5, num_leaves=24, feature_fraction=0.8, bagging_fraction=0.8, and 
early stopping of 30 rounds on the validation partition. LightGBM outputs conditional class probabilities P(Up) in [0, 1]. No 
post-hoc probability calibration (Platt scaling or isotonic regression) is applied. 
3.2 Tier 2: NSGA-II Multi-Objective Evolutionary Engine 
The evolutionary decision layer optimizes a 7-dimensional real-valued chromosome: g = [p_entry, p_exit, SL, TP, Trail, VolAlloc, 
MaxHold], bounded strictly by domain constraints: p_entry in [0.52, 0.85], p_exit in [0.35, 0.50], Stop-Loss multiplier SL in [1.0, 
4.5] x ATR, Take-Profit multiplier TP in [1.5, 7.0] x ATR, Trailing Stop percentage Trail in [0.01, 0.06], Volatility discount 
allocation fraction VolAlloc in [0.10, 1.00], and Maximum holding duration MaxHold in [5, 45] bars. 
NSGA-II evaluates each candidate chromosome across the 2-year Development partition under two conflicting objective 
functions: (1) Maximize Annualized Sharpe Ratio f_1 = (mu_r / sigma_r) * sqrt(252) (assuming a 0.0% risk-free rate); (2) 
Minimize Maximum Drawdown f_2 = max_t ((Peak_t - Equity_t) / Peak_t). To discourage inactivity during evolutionary search, 
candidate rules generating fewer than 5 completed trades during development receive a penalty: Sharpe is decremented by 
2.0 (Sharpe -= 2.0) and MaxDrawdown is floored at 0.5 (MaxDD = max(MaxDD, 0.5)). The population of size N=30 evolves 
across G=10 generations using Simulated Binary Crossover (SBX, crossover probability pc=0.85, eta_c=15) and Polynomial 
Mutation (per-gene mutation probability pm = 1/7, eta_m=20). Following development search, the individual with the highest 
development Sharpe ratio is selected. If no candidate achieves >= 5 development trades, the system defaults to 100% Cash 
during out-of-sample evaluation. 
3.3 Causal Simulator & Execution Mechanics 
The backtest engine enforces realistic causal timing: (1) Next-Bar Execution: Signals evaluated at bar t Close execute strictly at 
bar t+1 Open at price P_entry = Open_{t+1} * (1 + slip); (2) Transaction Friction: Standard costs are 0.05% commission and 
0.05% slippage per side (approximately 10 bps per side, 20 bps round-trip); sensitivity tests double friction to 0.10% per side 
(approximately 40 bps round-trip); (3) Conservative Intrabar Priority: If both stop-loss and take-profit price levels are touched 
within the same session's high-low range, the simulator prioritizes the stop-loss; (4) Opening Gaps: Gaps below stop-loss or 
above take-profit fill at the opening price with slippage penalty; (5) Zero-Volume Prohibition: No trade fills occur on zero-
volume sessions; (6) Scheduled Fold-End Policy: New entries are barred on the scheduled last tradable session (i < 
last_tradable_idx); open positions liquidate at final session Close.

5.3 Annual Paired Strategy Differences & Scorecard 
Evaluating the 18 annual folds individually reveals the structural asymmetry between annual drawdown dampening and 
secular compounding: (1) Positive Hybrid Return: Achieved in 10 out of 18 folds (55.6%); (2) Return Outperformance vs Buy & 
Hold: Hybrid exceeded Buy & Hold in 5 out of 18 folds (27.8%)—specifically during market corrections in 2022 (TCS +16.55% 
diff, INFY +11.44% diff), 2023 (RELIANCE +2.00% diff, TCS +3.92% diff), and 2024 (RELIANCE +1.97% diff); (3) Buy & Hold 
Outperformance: Buy & Hold exceeded Hybrid in 13 out of 18 folds (72.2%), particularly during strong bull markets such as 
2020 (INFY B&H +74.69% vs Hyb +27.83%) and 2021 (INFY B&H +52.73% vs Hyb +17.22%); (4) Maximum Drawdown Reduction: 
Hybrid exhibited lower annual maximum drawdown in 17 out of 18 folds (94.4%). The sole exception was RELIANCE.NS in 
2022, where Buy & Hold had a 17.27% drawdown vs Hybrid median drawdown of 17.45%. 
5.4 Ablation and Exposure-Matched Diagnostic 
To isolate the contribution of the ML classifier, a Constant Probability Ablation (p=0.60) evolved rules omitting LightGBM 
features. The Ablation achieved 6-year stitched returns of +42.42% for RELIANCE.NS (vs Hyb -0.35%), +47.37% for TCS.NS (vs 
Hyb +25.60%), and +18.69% for INFY.NS (vs Hyb +9.10%). Furthermore, an Exposure-Matched Buy & Hold diagnostic blending 
passive holding with cash based on the Hybrid's development exposure fraction (w_dev) outperformed the Full Hybrid in 29 
out of 54 runs (53.7%), while the Hybrid outperformed in 22 runs (40.7%), with 3 ties (RELIANCE 2020 cash fallbacks). 
Exposure-Matched Buy & Hold exhibited lower maximum drawdown than the Full Hybrid in 43 of 54 runs (79.6%). This 
diagnostic assumes daily frictionless rebalancing and 0% cash return. These findings confirm that technical metaheuristic rules 
provide strong risk containment, but LightGBM probability hurdles restricted market participation without adding net excess 
return.

5.5 Transaction Cost Sensitivity & Slippage Dynamics 
Evaluating frozen Development chromosomes under doubled transaction friction (0.10% commission + 0.10% slippage per 
side, 40 bps round-trip) caused substantial performance drops: RELIANCE.NS Seed 42 total return declined from -6.58% to -
25.41% (-18.83 pp); TCS.NS Seed 43 declined from +25.60% to +19.61% (-5.99 pp); and INFY.NS Seed 44 dropped from 
+27.88% to -7.97% (-35.85 pp). Mechanistically, slippage does not act as a simple linear fee. Because execution price shifts 
upward (P_entry = Open * (1 + slip)), the dynamic ATR stop-loss threshold (P_stop = P_entry - SL * ATR) is mechanically 
elevated. On sessions where prices touch the stop band, trades exit prematurely, altering subsequent cash levels and 
eliminating future trade signals. 
6. Discussion, Risk Attribution & Methodological Limitations 
The empirical findings highlight several critical trade-offs in quantitative portfolio design: (1) Asymmetric Risk-Return Profile: 
NSGA-II successfully discovers defensive chromosome parameters that truncate downside drawdowns during sharp 
corrections. However, because the classifier's directional probabilities hover narrowly around 0.50, high entry thresholds 
cause the system to remain in cash during multi-month bull market regimes, resulting in heavy compounding penalties relative 
to passive index compounding. (2) Multi-Year vs Annual Drawdown Asymmetry: Lower annual drawdowns in 17 of 18 folds do 
not guarantee lower multi-year risk. On INFY.NS, the Hybrid 6-year maximum drawdown reached 37.16% compared to 35.93% 
for Buy & Hold, illustrating how compounded capital erosion can produce deeper multi-year drawdowns. (3) Capital Exposure 
as an Overnight Proxy: In our evaluation, capital exposure is defined as session-close invested value divided by equity 
(Shares_t * Close_t / Equity_t). This proxy reflects overnight capital commitment and omits intraday capital utilization for 
same-day round trips. (4) Survivorship and Single-Asset Constraints: The study evaluates three surviving large-cap equities 
individually without cross-asset portfolio diversification. (5) Retrospective Protocol Adjustments: While data snapshots and 
random seeds remained strictly frozen, the final experimental protocol incorporated retrospective corrections (versioned in 
protocol_correction.json v1.1.0) following initial simulator inspection. No formal statistical significance tests are asserted; 
results represent retrospective empirical observations. 
7. Conclusion 
This research evaluated the integration of gradient tree boosting (LightGBM) with multi-objective evolutionary optimization 
(NSGA-II) for algorithmic trading across three major Indian equities over 2020–2025. Across 18 annual walk-forward folds, the 
LightGBM classifier demonstrated modest predictive capability (AUC 0.5324), tracking majority-class baselines. While NSGA-II 
evolved robust risk-management rules that lowered annual maximum drawdowns in 94.4% of folds, passive Buy & Hold 
generated substantially higher multi-year compounded wealth across all assets. Ablation analysis confirmed that 
metaheuristic technical parameter search without ML signals provided equivalent or superior capital growth. These results 
provide an honest empirical caution against assuming that machine learning directional predictors consistently improve 
quantitative trading strategies, highlighting the paramount role of opportunity cost in risk-constrained portfolio systems.
